Yes. Foreign nationals can own and run businesses in Armenia, and the registration process is among the more straightforward in the region.

## Choosing a structure

Two forms cover most cases.

**Individual entrepreneur** suits freelancers, consultants, and one-person operations. Registration is quick and ongoing administration is light. The trade-off is that you and the business are not legally separate.

**Limited liability company** suits anything with partners, employees, outside investment, or meaningful commercial risk. It separates your personal assets from the business, at the cost of more formality in reporting.

The right answer usually follows from three questions: are there other owners, will you take on liabilities, and do you need to look substantial to clients or investors.

## What registration involves

- Selecting and checking the availability of a company name
- Preparing founding documents and identifying the director
- Providing a registered legal address
- Filing with the state registry
- Receiving registration data and a tax identification number
- Opening a corporate bank account

Registration itself is typically fast once the documents are correct. Banking is often the slower step, since banks conduct their own compliance checks on foreign founders.

## Points foreigners tend to miss

You will need a genuine legal address, not merely a mailing one. Company documents are filed in Armenian. Some activities require licences beyond the basic registration. And once registered, reporting obligations begin immediately, whether or not the business has started trading.

## Can it be done remotely?

In some circumstances, yes, by power of attorney. Whether it is possible in your case depends on the structure you choose, your nationality, and current requirements from the registry and the banks. It is worth confirming before you plan travel around it.
