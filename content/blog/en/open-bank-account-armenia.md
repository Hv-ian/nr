A local bank account makes daily life considerably easier: paying rent, receiving income, settling utilities, and meeting the requirements of various administrative procedures.

## What you will generally need

- A valid passport
- A tax identification number
- Proof of address, in Armenia or abroad depending on the bank
- Documentation of your source of funds or income
- In some cases, a residence card

Requirements differ between banks and change over time. It is worth checking with the specific branch before making the trip.

## Choosing a bank

The Armenian banking sector is competitive, and several banks actively serve foreign clients with English-language service and functional mobile apps. Points worth comparing:

- Whether accounts can be opened without residency
- Availability of multi-currency accounts, which most newcomers want
- International transfer fees and correspondent arrangements
- Card issuance times and whether cards work reliably abroad
- Quality of the app, which varies more than you might expect

## The process

Account opening is usually done in person and can often be completed the same day, with the card following shortly after. The bank will run compliance checks, and this is where timelines vary. Foreign clients, and anyone with a more complex financial background, should expect more questions rather than fewer.

Be ready to explain, with documents, where your money comes from. Vague answers are the most common cause of delay.

## Some practical notes

Armenian banks handle multiple currencies routinely, which is useful if you are paid from abroad. Cash remains common in daily life, though card acceptance in Yerevan is now widespread. Keep your tax number to hand, as you will be asked for it often.
