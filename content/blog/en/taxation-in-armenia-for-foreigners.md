Tax is where relocation plans most often meet reality. The rules are not complicated, but they are specific, and they depend heavily on your residency status rather than your nationality.

> Rates and thresholds change. Treat the structure below as the map, and confirm current figures with a specialist before making decisions.

## Tax residency comes first

Armenia, like most countries, taxes based on residency rather than citizenship. Residency is generally determined by physical presence over the tax year, or by where your centre of vital interests sits. Once you are a tax resident, your worldwide income comes into scope. Before that, generally only Armenian-source income does.

This single distinction drives most of the planning. If you are moving mid-year, the timing of your arrival can matter.

## What individuals pay

Employment income is taxed at a flat rate, withheld by the employer along with mandatory social contributions. Income from other sources, including foreign income once you are resident, is declared through an annual return.

## What businesses pay

The regime depends on scale and structure. Smaller operations, including many individual entrepreneurs, may qualify for a simplified turnover-based regime. Larger companies fall under standard profit tax, with VAT applying above a registration threshold.

There are also incentives worth knowing about, particularly for technology businesses and exporters, which can materially change the arithmetic.

## Double taxation

Armenia has treaties with a substantial number of countries. If your home country also claims taxing rights, a treaty usually determines which one prevails and how relief is given. Check whether your country has one before assuming you will be taxed twice.

## Practical obligations

Registration for a tax number, periodic filings, payroll reporting if you employ anyone, and an annual return where applicable. Deadlines are enforced and penalties accrue, so the calendar matters as much as the calculation.
