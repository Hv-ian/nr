Most newcomers to Armenia settle in Yerevan, and for good reasons. But it is not the only option, and it is worth knowing what the alternatives offer.

## Yerevan

The capital holds the overwhelming majority of the country's international community, its jobs, its coworking spaces, its international schools, and its embassies. It is also where nearly all administrative procedures happen.

Within the city, character varies a lot. Kentron is central, walkable, and expensive. Arabkir is quieter and residential, popular with families. Areas further out trade proximity for space and lower rent.

If you are moving for work or setting up a business, Yerevan is the default, and usually the right one.

## Gyumri

Armenia's second city has a distinct identity, a strong artistic tradition, and noticeably lower costs. It has attracted a small but real community of remote workers and creative professionals. The trade-off is a thinner service layer and fewer international options.

## Dilijan

Set in forested mountains, Dilijan is quiet, green, and increasingly known internationally, in part through the presence of an international school. It suits people who want nature and calm and do not need a city on their doorstep. It is a couple of hours from Yerevan.

## Vanadzor

The third-largest city, with reasonable infrastructure and low costs. Less oriented toward newcomers than the others, but a practical choice for those wanting a normal Armenian city rather than an expat enclave.

## Choosing

The practical test is what you need weekly. If that includes an office, international schooling, or frequent flights, choose Yerevan. If it is good internet, mountains, and a lower cost base, the others become genuinely competitive.

Many people begin in Yerevan while their paperwork is processed, then move once they know the country better. That is a sensible order.

> **Keep in mind:** Requirements and procedures can change. It is always best to check the latest information or get professional assistance.
